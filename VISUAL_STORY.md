# Visual story standard

Every product page and dashboard widget should earn its space by helping a
person make sense of something real. Apply this standard to the personal
platform (Finance, Library, Athletic Analytics, 14ers, and the main site).

1. Begin with one human question, stated in the heading. Examples: “Where did
   the money go?”, “Am I stronger than a year ago?”, or “What changed in my
   training?”
2. Lead with the smallest useful visual answer. Use a chart only when it makes
   time, composition, comparison, uncertainty, or cause-and-effect easier to
   understand than a sentence or number.
3. Explain the answer in plain language. State the period, definition,
   uncertainty, and meaningful caveats close to the visual rather than hiding
   them in a tooltip.
4. Make the supporting evidence available without making the overview a wall
   of numbers: use an intentional drilldown, expandable evidence, or a focused
   linked view.
5. Offer an appropriate next action. It can be learning more, correcting data,
   recording feedback, or opening a relevant tool—never an unexplained
   financial or account-changing action.
6. Preserve the same information on small screens. Reorder, collapse evidence,
   and simplify interaction for touch; do not rely on hover, tiny tables, or
   horizontal page scrolling.
7. Keep forecasts visibly separate from observed facts. Name the assumptions,
   source date, and confidence; never imply an estimate is a guarantee,
   appraisal, tax advice, or a prediction.

Before shipping, check the page at a desktop and phone width. A person should
be able to identify the question, visual answer, evidence, and next action
without needing to decode a metric wall.
